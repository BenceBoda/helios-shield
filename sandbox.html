Estimated geoelectric field over the Hungarian grid

The quantity that drives GIC is the electric field induced in the ground, not dB/dt itself. Here it is estimated from Tihany magnetometer data and a published layered ground model of the Pannonian Basin, then projected onto the 220–750 kV lines mapped in OpenStreetMap. A first step towards a local model. It is not a GIC forecast.

How it is computed

  • Magnetic data: Tihany (THY) one-minute observatory data via INTERMAGNET / BGS GIN. Live: last 24 hours, recomputed every 10 minutes.
  • Ground: one-dimensional layer sequences No 1 and No 3 for the Pannonian Basin from Ádám, Szarka, Novák and Wesztergom, “Key results on deep electrical conductivity anomalies in the Pannonian Basin”, Table 1. The third option is a uniform 1000 Ωm ground as used for the Czech grid in this study, shown only to compare: it is not a Hungarian model.
  • Method: plane-wave surface impedance of the layer stack, applied to the magnetic variations in the frequency domain (the standard approach in GIC studies).
  • Lines: 220, 400 and 750 kV ways from OpenStreetMap (© contributors, ODbL). Colour = field component along the line; the popup gives the voltage across that mapped section.

What this is not

  • Not GIC. Current also depends on transformer and earthing resistances and the network topology, which public data do not give.
  • One station and one regional model for the whole country: real ground varies locally, and the choice of model changes the answer several-fold (compare the reference model).
  • One-minute data underestimate the fastest variations.
